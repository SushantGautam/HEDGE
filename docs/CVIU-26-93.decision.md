Reviewers' comments:



AE:
Two experts reviewed the manuscript. The reviewers have raised a number of important concerns, including especially the unclear motivation, lack of sufficient literature review, lack of thorough evaluations on datasets outside of medical domains, and missing baselines, among others. Overall, although the problems being addressed are potentially of interest to our readership, your manuscript, in its current form, does not meet the required quality standards to be considered for publication.


Reviewer #1: Summary This paper proposes HEDGE, a unified framework for hallucination detection in Vision-Language Models. The method integrates controlled visual perturbations, semantic clustering, and uncertainty metrics to estimate "dense geometric entropy." The authors conduct extensive experiments on medical VQA datasets (VQA-RAD and KvasirVQA-x1) using three different VLM architectures to validate the framework.

Strengths

Novel Perspective: The motivation to shift from token-level uncertainty to geometric stability offers an interesting and promising perspective for understanding VLM reliability.

Comprehensive Framework: The proposed framework rigorously considers multiple critical factors in hallucination detection, including sampling strategies and distortion synthesis.

Robust Empirical Validation: The authors perform extensive experiments to validate their claims across different settings and prompt configurations.

Generalizability: The evaluation covers diverse model architectures (LLaVA-Med, Med-Gemma, Qwen2.5-VL), demonstrating the applicability of the framework across different model types.

Weaknesses 1. Unclear Methodological Motivation: The logical connection between the proposed components is not fully justified. Specifically, why does the framework necessitate the specific combination of visual perturbations, semantic clustering, and uncertainty metrics? The authors should clarify the theoretical necessity of this complex design compared to simpler alternatives.

2. Narrow Literature Review: The analysis of limitations in Section 1.1 is too specific. It targets individual prior works (e.g., SE, RadFlag) rather than broader methodological categories. Furthermore, the claim that existing methods fail to address the issues mentioned in Lines 45-49 appears too absolute and requires verification. Some recent works may have already mitigated these limitations, and the authors should provide a more objective categorization.

3. Insufficient General Domain Evaluation: The experiments rely solely on medical datasets (VQA-RAD, KvasirVQA-x1). However, the paper claims to propose a "unified framework." To demonstrate general effectiveness, the authors must include experiments on mainstream general-domain benchmarks. I suggest adding comparisons on datasets such as POPE [1], PRE-HAL [2], or PhD [3].

[1] Li Y, et al. Evaluating object hallucination in large vision-language models. EMNLP 2023.

[2] Huang T, et al. Visual hallucination detection in large vision-language models via evidential conflict. IJAR 2025.

[3] Liu J, et al. PhD: A ChatGPT-Prompted Visual Hallucination Evaluation Dataset. CVPR 2025.

4. Ambiguity in Metric Selection: The paper utilizes SE, RadFlag, and VASE within the experimental section. However, it remains unclear which specific metric constitutes the core of "HEDGE" for practical application. The authors should clarify whether HEDGE is a specific scoring metric or merely a wrapper pipeline for existing metrics.

5. Missing Basic Baselines: The baseline comparison is incomplete. The authors should include standard, fundamental uncertainty metrics such as Maximum Probability (MaxProb) and Predictive Entropy calculated on raw output tokens to provide a comprehensive performance reference.

6. Lack of Synthesized Insights: While the experimental section presents a large volume of data, the analysis is somewhat fragmented. It is difficult to quickly grasp the core takeaways. I suggest adding a summary table that lists key experimental phenomena and corresponding conclusions. This would significantly improve the readability and provide clearer guidance for future research.

7. Discussion of Emerging Literature: To further strengthen the paper's positioning, I recommend discussing some very recent and highly relevant works, such as [4-6]. Although some of these might have appeared concurrently with this submission, contrasting HEDGE with these approaches would provide valuable context for the readers.

[4] Xiong M, Hu Z, Lu X, et al. Can LLMs Express Their Uncertainty? An Empirical Evaluation of Confidence Elicitation in LLMs. ICLR 2025.

[5] Park S, Li S. GLSim: Detecting object hallucinations in LVLMs via global-local similarity. NeurIPS 2025.

[6] Huang T, Wang R, Liu X, et al. Detecting Misbehaviors of Large Vision-Language Models by Evidential Uncertainty Quantification. ICLR 2026.


Reviewer #2: There is no separate graphical abstract in the manuscript. Figure 1 shows the flow of the HEDGE framework from medical image input through sampling, clustering (NLI-based and embedding-based), to uncertainty quantification and hallucination detection. However, this is an embedded methodology figure, not a purpose-designed graphical abstract. An appropriate graphical abstract for this paper should be an independent, aesthetically refined summary that encapsulates the primary contribution, which is HEDGE's integration of visual perturbations, semantic clustering, and geometric entropy metrics for detecting VLM hallucinations, alongside the principal finding that VASE, in conjunction with embedding clustering at moderate sampling budgets, yields the most reliable hallucination signal. There is no such specific visual overview.

Also, there is no section for highlights. For a journal like CVIU, the authors should give about 3-5 short bullet points. Some good highlights for this paper are: the introduction of HEDGE as a single, domain-agnostic way to find hallucinations; the systematic comparison of NLI-based and embedding-based clustering; the finding that VASE is the most reliable metric, especially with moderate sampling budgets of 10-15 samples; the demonstration that architecture and visual tokenization density have a big effect on how easy it is to find hallucinations; and the release of the hedge-bench PyPI library for reproducible benchmarking.
