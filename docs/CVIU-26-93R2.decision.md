Editor's comments:



AE:
Three experts reviewed the manuscript, among them, one is the existing reviewer, and the other two reviewers are newly invited to offer a balanced review, since the other existing review has been unable to finish after an extended period of time.
A number of important concerns have been raised by the two new reviewers, including but not limited to, technical contributions; overclaim concern; empirical evaluations such as dataset split, clinician verification, statistical analysis; presentation issues such as clear definition of key concepts and possibly tune down some claim.
Overall, although the problems being addressed are potentially of interest to our readership, your manuscript, in its current form, does not meet the required quality standards to be considered for publication.


Reviewer #1: The authors have comprehensively addressed all the concerns and suggestions raised in the previous round of review. The revised manuscript provides a more profound analysis of the proposed HEDGE (Hallucination Estimation via Dense Geometric Entropy) framework.
Given the substantial improvements and the technical soundness of the current version, I believe the manuscript now meets the standards of Computer Vision and Image Understanding. Consequently, I recommend the paper for acceptance in its current form.


Reviewer #4: 1. The research content of the paper is not clear enough, the structure of the paper is redundant, and the main innovative points are not prominent. This framework integrates sampling, visual perturbation, semantic clustering, and uncertainty measurement, but the research content is too chaotic and lacks clarity and rigor in describing core concepts and key experimental designs.

2. The paper has a vague definition of "dense geometric entropy", which has not been strictly defined or formalized. It is more like a metaphorical term used to describe the instability of clustering response, rather than a computable and verifiable mathematical quantity.

3. The label generation method for "illusion" relies on a single LLM and lacks robustness verification. All ground truth labels for the experiments were generated by a single LLM (Qwen3-30B-A3B) as the 'medical arbiter'. LLM adjudicators themselves may have systematic biases similar to the VLM being evaluated, leading to inflated or underestimated evaluation results. The paper does not provide any human expert validation, multi arbitrator consistency analysis (such as Kappa coefficient), or quantification of arbitrator uncertainty.

4. Insufficient support for 'domain agnostic'. The author claims that HEDGE is' domain independent ', but the main experiments are focused entirely on medical VQA (VQA RAD, Kvasir VQA x1). Although a POPE (non-medical) experiment has been added (Section 5.8), the scale of this experiment is too small (only one model and one dataset), and the results (VASE AUC ≈ 0.70) are much lower than the performance on medical datasets (>0.85), indicating that the effectiveness of the framework may significantly decrease in non-medical and more open scenarios.


Reviewer #6: Summary: This revision improves the presentation, expands the related work, and adds uncertainty baselines and a non-medical experiment. However, several fundamental concerns remain regarding methodological novelty, possible test-set leakage, reliability of hallucination labels, and unsupported architectural interpretations. These issues directly affect the reasonability of the publication and they cannot be addressed through minor revision.

Comments:
1. HEDGE mainly integrates existing perturbation, clustering, SE, RadFlag, and VASE components. "Dense geometric entropy" lacks an independent formulation, so the methodological novelty remains limited.
2. The embedding threshold is optimized using ROC-AUC, but no separate validation split is clearly defined. This creates a serious risk of test-set leakage and conflicts with the claim that the threshold is fixed before testing.
3. Hallucination labels are generated by a single LLM adjudicator without clinician verification or agreement analysis. Therefore, the reported results may reflect agreement with the judge rather than reliable hallucination detection.
4. The architectural conclusions are insufficiently supported, since HEDGE does not directly analyze internal VLM representations. Moreover, the limited POPE experiment is inadequate to establish domain-agnostic generalization.
5. Confidence intervals and significance tests are missing, while sampling scale and perturbation budget are coupled. Several practical statements are also inconsistent, such as describing RadFlag as requiring only one forward pass. These issues weaken the reliability of the conclusions.

Decision:
Reject
Although this revision has improved the quality of the manuscript, I believe the key issues outlined above should be properly addressed before resubmission.
